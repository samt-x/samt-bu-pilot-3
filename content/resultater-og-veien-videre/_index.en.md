The pilot started in summer 2026 and runs until the end of November 2026. It has not made a POC or MVP. The results so far are the mapping, the choice of track and the legal clarification of consent. The sources are the findings after the workshops (version 0.2, 8 September 2026), the demos on 4 September, 16 October and 6 November, and the memo on consent.

## Results so far

- **The current situation has been mapped** in Oslo and Nittedal: no common routines, grades and absence are sent as PDF, the pupil file is not categorised, and consent is obtained in many different ways. See [Current situation](/en/pilotering/pilot-3/naasituasjon/).
- **Four tracks have been assessed, and one has been chosen:** the routines are proposed to KS, the standard for transfer to Pilot 1, the consent solution is lifted out, and the pilot goes further with AI support for reviewing the pupil file. See [Alternative solutions](/en/pilotering/pilot-3/loesningsvalg/alternative-loesninger/).
- **Consent has been clarified:** what the school can share without consent, what requires it, and that the consent is not a legal basis for processing under the GDPR. See [Legal matters](/en/pilotering/pilot-3/juridiske-forhold/).
- **Privacy as part of the choice:** the pilot has shown how nature, scope, purpose and context decide how thorough the assessments a track requires are. This was presented at an informal SAMT-BU demo session and will be presented to Digdir's user council on 1–2 December 2026.
- **Plan for continuation** with three steps and resource needs. See [Roadmap](/en/pilotering/pilot-3/loesningsvalg/veikart/).

## Lessons learned

- Municipalities handle moves very differently, so it was right to start with a thorough mapping before the solution was chosen.
- What is experienced as a digitalisation problem may just as well be a problem with routines. Not everything should be solved with a new solution.
- Privacy should be included when the tracks are chosen. It then becomes clear earlier what is possible, and how much must be assessed before a solution can be put into use.
- A short pilot cannot solve the most risky challenges on its own. There must be a balance between ambition, risk and resources, and the work can be built up step by step over several pilots.
- The pilot has not had access to technical AI resources, so the technical work must wait.

## The way forward

Within the pilot period, the legal assessment continues, and the pilot will contact Oslo and Nittedal about school staff who can help with the categories. The pilot is also assessing the consent solutions of Digdir and KS Digital. A continuation in 2027 has been planned and estimated, but not decided. See [Roadmap](/en/pilotering/pilot-3/loesningsvalg/veikart/).
