How municipalities handle a pupil moving during the school year today, as it emerged in the workshops with Oslo and Nittedal in August 2026.

## Data collection

The project group held three workshops in weeks 34–36: two with the City of Oslo and one with Nittedal. From Oslo, school administration staff took part. From Nittedal, both school administration staff and teachers from primary and lower secondary school took part. The workshops looked at the move from three angles: practical routines, data (which data are transferred, systems and formats) and law (what requires consent, and how consent is obtained in practice). The findings were collected and structured on a board in Miro.

## Routines

There are no fixed, common routines for pupils who move, neither where the pupil moves from nor where the pupil moves to. Schools in the same municipality may have their own local routines, and much is manual practice with varying degrees of documentation. How much the school staff must help depends on the parents' digital skills and language skills.

When asked what would make the move easier, the municipalities answered that better routines, guidelines and examples of best practice would help a lot.

## Data that are transferred

Two types of information are sent to the new school:

- **Standard information about the pupil** from the school administration system. For lower secondary school, this is grades and absence. They are sent as PDF documents, not as structured data, so the new school must type them into its own system.
- **Other documents about the pupil** from the pupil file in the case and archive system, also as PDF. For most pupils there are few or no such documents. For some there are hundreds. An estimated five to twelve per cent of pupils need adapted education, and that is often when many documents come along.

The shipment is sent with Fiks SvarUt, which all municipalities in Norway use. Schools that have set up the receiving system Fiks SvarInn get the shipment directly into the line-of-business system. Schools without SvarInn get it in their Altinn inbox and must retrieve it from there.

## Review of the pupil file

Once consent has been obtained, the school the pupil moves from must assess which documents in the pupil file are relevant to the new school. The documents are not labelled or categorised, so there is no simple way to filter them. For pupils with many documents, this is time-consuming and difficult. Sometimes almost everything is sent, to be sure that nothing relevant is left out. Sensitive documents that should not have been sent may then remain in the pupil file at both the old and the new school.

## Consent

Information that cannot be shared without consent under the Education Act (opplæringslova) (see [Legal matters](/en/pilotering/pilot-3/juridiske-forhold/)) requires consent from the parents. The municipalities use many different solutions to obtain it:

- consent solutions in childhood and education administration systems
- consent solutions the municipality has made itself
- Posten's signing solution
- paper forms

The consent templates are made locally in each municipality, not nationally. Because of the parents' digital skills and language skills, obtaining consent in some cases requires a lot of follow-up from teachers or school administration.
